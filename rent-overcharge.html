Rent overcharge claims for rent-stabilized tenants in Queens and New York City

Written and reviewed by Jason Berke, Esq., Law Offices of Jason Berke PC, Kew Gardens, Queens · Updated November 25, 2026

Our experience

We represented a rent-stabilized tenant in a DHCR rent overcharge proceeding in Brooklyn. The Rent Administrator found that the apartment’s rent history reflected a fraudulent scheme to deregulate the apartment, set the base date rent using DHCR’s default formula, and found the overcharge willful because the owner had not shown otherwise. DHCR awarded the tenant $51,696 in overcharges, $51,696 in treble damages, $21,639.65 in interest, and $1,077 in excess security, a total of $126,108.65 before credit for rent the tenant had withheld. The owner was also directed to roll back the rent.

If you live in a rent-stabilized apartment, your landlord may charge no more than the legal regulated rent. When the rent charged is higher, the tenant may be entitled to a rent rollback, a refund, and in some cases three times the overcharge plus attorney’s fees. What is a rent overcharge?

A rent-stabilized apartment has a legal regulated rent: a starting rent plus the increases the law allows, such as Rent Guidelines Board lease renewal increases and certain approved improvement increases. Owners must register the rent with the New York State Division of Housing and Community Renewal (DHCR). A rent overcharge occurs when the owner collects more than the legal regulated rent. Common sources include rent increases not permitted by law, increases based on improvements that were not performed or documented, an apartment treated as deregulated when it was still stabilized, and missing or unreliable rent registrations. DHCR can order an owner to lower the rent and refund the excess. HCR: Rent Increases and Rent Overcharge.

Is my apartment rent stabilized?

Start with your lease and renewal leases; a stabilized lease should include a rent stabilization rider. You can request the apartment’s registration history from HCR online or by calling HCR’s Office of Rent Administration at (833) 499-0343. HCR mails the history to the apartment address, generally within about 20 business days. If the apartment was never registered as stabilized, you may receive nothing. NYC notes that buildings with six or more units built before 1974 are more likely to be stabilized, but some newer buildings are also covered, and coverage questions are often disputed. NYC rent increase guide.

How the 2019 rent law changed overcharge claims

The Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (HSTPA), effective June 14, 2019, rewrote the overcharge provisions of the Rent Stabilization Law (RSL). Under the current text of RSL § 26-516(a) and CPLR 213-a:

  • Filing deadline and lookback. A complaint may be filed with DHCR or in court at any time, but recovery is limited to the six years before the complaint is filed.
  • Base rent. The legal regulated rent is generally the rent in the most recent reliable annual registration statement filed and served six or more years before the most recent registration statement, plus later lawful increases. DHCR or the court must consider all available rent history reasonably necessary to decide the claim.
  • Treble damages. The owner is liable for three times the overcharge unless it proves by a preponderance of the evidence that the overcharge was not willful. Treble damages may be assessed on willful overcharges going back six years. A refund or rent adjustment made after the complaint is filed and served is not evidence that the overcharge was not willful.
  • Fees and interest. An owner found to have overcharged shall be assessed the reasonable costs and attorney’s fees of the proceeding and interest from the date of the overcharge.

Older overcharges: Regina and the fraud exception

In Matter of Regina Metro. Co., LLC v New York State Div. of Hous. & Community Renewal, 35 NY3d 332 (2020), the Court of Appeals held that the HSTPA’s overcharge calculation amendments cannot be applied retroactively to overcharges that occurred before their enactment. For those overcharges, the prior rules apply: generally, the rent charged four years before the complaint is the base date rent, and rental history before that date is not examined.

The main exception is fraud. Where a tenant presents evidence of a fraudulent scheme to deregulate the apartment or evade rent stabilization, older rent history may be reviewed to decide whether fraud occurred, and if it did, the base date rent may be set by DHCR’s “default formula” rather than the tainted registered rent. In Burrows v 75-25 153rd St., LLC, 44 NY3d 74 (2025), the Court of Appeals clarified that a tenant need not show reasonable reliance on the owner’s misrepresentation for the fraud exception to apply; the tenant must allege sufficient indicia of fraud. The Legislature has also amended the rent laws on establishing fraud (L 2023, ch 760; L 2024, ch 95), and courts have applied those amendments to claims pending as of December 22, 2023. Burrows v 75-25 153rd St., LLC, 244 AD3d 408 (1st Dept 2025). Which rules govern a particular claim depends on when the overcharges occurred and when the claim was filed.

DHCR complaint or court case?

DHCR. A rent-stabilized tenant files an overcharge complaint on Form RA-89 or online through Rent Connect, with supporting documents such as leases, rent receipts, and cancelled checks. The owner is served and may respond, and DHCR may request more records from both sides. A Rent Administrator’s order can set the legal rent and direct a rollback and refund. Either side may challenge the order by filing a Petition for Administrative Review (PAR) within 35 days of issuance. Once an overcharge order is final, the tenant may generally collect the award by deducting up to 20% of it from each month’s rent or, in some circumstances, by filing the order as a judgment. HCR Fact Sheet #16.

Court. An overcharge can also be raised in a court of competent jurisdiction. A tenant sued in a nonpayment proceeding may raise the overcharge as a defense and counterclaim in Housing Court, although a judge can leave the overcharge question for DHCR to decide. Tenants may also bring an overcharge action in Supreme Court, as the tenants did in Burrows. NY Courts: Rent overcharge in a rent-stabilized apartment.

The forums differ in speed, discovery, how fees are pursued, and whether related claims can be joined. If a court case is already pending, get advice before filing a separate DHCR complaint.

What documents should I gather?

  • Your first lease, every renewal lease, and all riders, including any rent stabilization or preferential rent rider.
  • Proof of each rent payment: cancelled checks, bank statements, money order receipts, or payment app records.
  • The DHCR rent registration history for your apartment.
  • Any notices about rent increases, apartment improvements, major capital improvements, or deregulation.
  • Any DHCR orders, prior complaints, or court papers involving the apartment or building.
  • A short timeline: move-in date, each rent amount, and any renovations you saw before or after moving in.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my apartment is rent stabilized?

Check your lease and renewal leases for a rent stabilization rider or references to rent stabilization. Then request the apartment’s rent registration history from New York State Homes and Community Renewal (HCR). NYC says an apartment is more likely to be stabilized if the building has six or more units and was built before 1974, but those facts alone do not decide coverage, and some newer buildings are stabilized. NYC rent increase guide.

How far back can a rent overcharge claim reach?

For complaints governed by the 2019 amendments, a complaint may be filed at any time, but recovery is limited to the six years before the complaint is filed. To find the legal rent, DHCR or the court starts from the most recent reliable registration filed and served six or more years before the most recent registration statement and may consider all available rent history reasonably necessary to decide the claim. RSL § 26-516(a); CPLR 213-a. Overcharges that occurred before June 14, 2019 are generally measured under the prior four-year rules (see Regina, below).

What are treble damages in a rent overcharge case?

An owner found to have collected an overcharge is liable for three times the overcharge unless the owner proves by a preponderance of the evidence that the overcharge was not willful; in that case the penalty is the overcharge plus interest. Under current law, treble damages apply to willful overcharges collected starting six years before the complaint. A voluntary refund or rent adjustment made after the complaint is filed and served is not evidence that the overcharge was not willful. RSL § 26-516(a), (a)(2).

Can I recover attorney’s fees?

Under RSL § 26-516(a)(4), an owner found to have overcharged shall be assessed the reasonable costs and attorney’s fees of the proceeding, plus interest from the date of the overcharge at the CPLR 5004 judgment rate. Whether and how much is awarded depends on the forum, the findings, and the record. RSL § 26-516(a)(4).

Should I file with DHCR or go to court?

Both are possible. A tenant can file a complaint with the Division of Housing and Community Renewal (DHCR) on Form RA-89 or through Rent Connect, or raise the overcharge in a court of competent jurisdiction, including as a defense or counterclaim in a Housing Court case or in a Supreme Court action. The RA-89 asks whether the same issue has been raised in court, and a Housing Court judge can leave an overcharge claim for DHCR to decide. The right choice depends on whether a case is already pending, the rent history, and the relief sought. HCR: Rent Increases and Rent Overcharge; NY Courts guide to rent overcharge in a stabilized apartment.

What should I bring to a consultation?

Your current lease, all renewal leases and riders, proof of rent paid (cancelled checks, bank statements, receipts, or payment app records), the DHCR rent registration history if you have it, any notices of rent increases or apartment improvements, and any DHCR orders or court papers. A short timeline of when you moved in and each rent change helps. Keep confidential documents for a later discussion after engagement is confirmed.

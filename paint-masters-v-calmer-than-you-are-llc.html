Paint Masters Auto Body Repairs Inc. v Calmer Than You Are LLC (Sup Ct, Kings County 2023): temporary restraints on a Yellowstone application

Written and reviewed by Jason Berke, Esq., Law Offices of Jason Berke PC, Kew Gardens, Queens · Last reviewed November 7, 2026

In Paint Masters Auto Body Repairs Inc. and John Carrington v. Calmer Than You Are LLC, Index No. 527520/2023, Supreme Court, Kings County, Jason Berke was counsel for the plaintiffs. On September 27, 2023, Justice Reginald A. Boddie signed an emergency order to show cause. Pending the hearing of the application for a preliminary injunction, the court enjoined and restrained the defendant, its agents, servants and employees, and all persons acting on its behalf from terminating the plaintiffs’ leasehold interest or lease, from commencing any summary or other proceeding to terminate or cancel the lease, and from attempting to remove the plaintiffs from possession, based upon the 30-day notice of termination. The application was returnable on October 26, 2023, at 9:30 a.m. The premises identified in the order are 723-749 Van Sinderen Avenue, Commercial Store #723, Brooklyn, New York 11207.

The parties then signed a stipulation of settlement dated October 25, 2023. By a Decision & Order dated October 26, 2023, and entered the same day as NYSCEF Document 19, Justice Boddie wrote: “Plaintiff’s motion (sequence one) for a preliminary injunction, or Yellowstone injunction is denied as moot based on the stipulation of settlement dated October 25, 2023 (NYSCEF Document No. 18).” The court so-ordered that stipulation, which provided that the defendant “is enjoined from terminating Plaintiff’s tenancy in anyway including but not limited to commencing a summary proceeding or ousting Plaintiffs in anyway from the premises located at 723-749 Van Sinderen Ave, Commercial Store #723 Brooklyn NY 11207 until January 1, 2024.”

What was the proceeding about?

The plaintiffs are Paint Masters Auto Body Repairs Inc. and John Carrington. The defendant is Calmer Than You Are LLC. Jason Berke, of Law Offices of Jason Berke PC, was counsel for the plaintiffs. He presented an emergency order to show cause seeking to permanently enjoin the defendant from terminating or cancelling the plaintiffs’ lease based upon a 30-day notice of termination, from commencing a summary holdover or other proceeding to recover possession of the commercial premises, from attempting to oust or remove the plaintiffs, and from taking any other action to terminate or cancel the leasehold based upon that notice. The court’s later Decision & Order described the motion as one “for a preliminary injunction, or Yellowstone injunction.”

What did the court order?

Justice Boddie signed the emergency order to show cause on September 27, 2023, as NYSCEF Document 17. It directed the defendant to show cause at IAS Part 12, Room 956, on October 26, 2023, at 9:30 a.m. Pending that hearing, the court ordered that the defendant, its agents, servants and employees, and all persons acting on its behalf “be and hereby are enjoined and restrained from terminating plaintiffs’ leasehold interest or lease, and from commencing any summary or other proceeding to terminate or cancel this lease, and attempting in any way to out and/or remove plaintiffs from possession, based upon the 30 day notice of termination.”

On October 25, 2023, counsel for both sides signed a stipulation of settlement, filed as NYSCEF Document 18. Jason Berke signed for the plaintiffs; Jaypreet Sahni, Esq., of JSS Law, P.C., signed for the defendant. The stipulation provided that the defendant is enjoined from terminating the plaintiff’s tenancy in any way, including but not limited to commencing a summary proceeding or ousting the plaintiffs from the premises at 723-749 Van Sinderen Avenue, Commercial Store #723, Brooklyn, New York 11207, until January 1, 2024. The parties consented to the stipulation being so-ordered.

By a Decision & Order dated October 26, 2023, entered that afternoon as NYSCEF Document 19, Justice Boddie denied the motion as moot based on that stipulation and so-ordered it. The Decision & Order states: “Plaintiff’s motion (sequence one) for a preliminary injunction, or Yellowstone injunction is denied as moot based on the stipulation of settlement dated October 25, 2023 (NYSCEF Document No. 18).”

What did the decision not decide?

  • The court did not grant a permanent injunction, and it did not decide the Yellowstone application on the merits. The October 26, 2023 Decision & Order denied the motion as moot because the parties had settled.
  • The September 27 restraints were temporary. They applied pending the hearing and were based upon the 30-day notice of termination.
  • The so-ordered settlement restraint ran only until January 1, 2024. It did not grant a continuing injunction after that date.
  • The signed order to show cause does not state the date of the 30-day notice of termination.

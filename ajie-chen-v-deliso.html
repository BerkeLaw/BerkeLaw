Ajie Chen v Deliso (App Div, 2d Dept 2019): intentional and negligent infliction of emotional distress claims dismissed

Written and reviewed by Jason Berke, Esq., Law Offices of Jason Berke PC, Kew Gardens, Queens · Last reviewed November 7, 2026

In Ajie Chen v Deliso, 2019 NY Slip Op 01054 [169 AD3d 761], decided February 13, 2019, the Appellate Division, Second Department reversed an order of Supreme Court, Kings County, and granted the defendant’s motion pursuant to CPLR 3211(a)(7) to dismiss the complaint. Accepting the plaintiffs’ allegations as true, the court held that they failed to state a cause of action for intentional or negligent infliction of emotional distress. Because those substantive claims were subject to dismissal, separately pleaded requests for future medical expenses, punitive damages, and a permanent injunction were also dismissed. The slip opinion was published by the New York State Law Reporting Bureau and corrected through March 27, 2019.

What was the dispute about?

The plaintiffs were the defendant’s neighbors. They sued to recover damages for intentional and negligent infliction of emotional distress, based on allegations that the defendant yelled obscenities and racial slurs at them. They also sought future medical expenses, punitive damages, and a permanent injunction. The defendant moved under CPLR 3211(a)(7) to dismiss the complaint. Supreme Court, Kings County, denied the motion, and the defendant appealed.

What did the court decide about intentional infliction of emotional distress?

Accepting the plaintiffs’ factual allegations as true, the Second Department held that the plaintiffs failed to state that cause of action. The court wrote that although the defendant’s alleged conduct was “deplorable and reprehensible,” it did not, as a matter of law, “arise to such an extreme or outrageous level as to meet the threshold requirements for intentional infliction of emotional distress.”

What did the court decide about negligent infliction of emotional distress?

The court held that the plaintiffs also failed to state that cause of action because they did not plead facts demonstrating “some guarantee of genuineness,” an element of negligent infliction of emotional distress.

What did the court decide about the other requested relief?

Because all of the substantive causes of action were subject to dismissal, the separately pleaded claims for future medical expenses, punitive damages, and a permanent injunction were also subject to dismissal. The court said that, in light of that conclusion, it need not reach the defendant’s remaining contentions.

What did the decision not decide?

  • The ruling came on a motion to dismiss for failure to state a cause of action. The court accepted the plaintiffs’ factual allegations as true for that purpose. It is not a trial verdict about what occurred.
  • The court did not hold that the alleged conduct was acceptable. It described that alleged conduct as “deplorable and reprehensible,” and decided only that the complaint did not meet the legal threshold for these tort claims.
  • The decision does not say that every claim based on slurs or verbal abuse must be dismissed. The outcome turned on how these claims were pleaded and on the legal standards the court applied to them.
  • The court did not reach the defendant’s remaining contentions.

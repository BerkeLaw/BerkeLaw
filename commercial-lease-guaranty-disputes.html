Commercial lease guaranty and unpaid rent disputes in Queens

Law Offices of Jason Berke PC, Kew Gardens, Queens · Updated November 8, 2026

A business can turn over the keys and still face a rent demand. A landlord may also seek payment from the person who signed the lease guaranty. We review the lease, the guaranty, and the record of the move-out to assess what each party may owe.

Owed rent after a commercial tenant moved out, or received a demand under a personal guaranty? Have the lease, signed guaranty, and any surrender correspondence ready when you contact us.

Can a landlord collect unpaid rent after a commercial tenant leaves?

Start by separating the tenant’s obligations under the lease from the guarantor’s obligations under the signed guaranty. A move-out can leave accrued rent unpaid, and the tenant may owe later charges depending on the lease, any surrender or release agreement, and how the tenancy ended. A guarantor’s obligation may end on a different date. If the tenant still occupies the premises and the landlord seeks possession, see our Queens commercial eviction guide.

Break down the demand by billing period and charge. Separate base rent, additional rent, interest, and legal fees, and check the contract terms supporting each amount. Account for payments, any security deposit applied, and credits under a settlement or the lease. Then compare the dates and charges with the obligations the guarantor actually agreed to cover.

What does a good-guy guaranty cover?

A good-guy guaranty is a limited commercial lease guaranty that commonly allows the guarantor’s exposure to later rent to end when the tenant surrenders the space and satisfies the agreed conditions. Covered charges that accrued before the cutoff may remain payable. The document may require advance written notice, delivery of keys, vacancy in a specified condition, payment through a defined date, or a signed surrender document. Some guaranties also address lease renewals, assignments, and amendments. Read the guaranty and the provisions of the lease it incorporates together. 1995 CAM LLC v West Side Advisors, LLC, 45 NY3d 150 (2025) and ROC-Lafayette Assoc., LLC v Sturm, 233 AD3d 418 (1st Dept 2024).

What counts as notice and surrender?

Check who had to receive notice, how it had to be delivered, and when the notice period began. Then record the actual move-out date, who received the keys, what remained in the space, and what the landlord said or did in response. A key receipt, walkthrough, dated photos, or written surrender agreement can help establish what occurred. Compare those records with every condition in the guaranty.

In 1995 CAM, New York’s Court of Appeals held that under the wording of that particular guaranty, the guarantor’s liability ended when the tenant gave the required notice, vacated, and returned the keys. The lease required the landlord’s written acceptance for the tenant to terminate the lease by surrender. The guaranty set a separate cutoff for the guarantor. The court reversed the award of post-move-out damages against the guarantor. The result turned on the language of the parties’ documents. Read the Court of Appeals decision in 1995 CAM.

What should I bring to a consultation?

  • The signed lease, riders, guaranty, renewals, assignments, and amendments.
  • Default and move-out notices, proof of delivery, and any surrender agreement or release.
  • Key receipts, walkthrough notes, dated photos, and messages about possession.
  • The rent ledger, invoices for additional rent, payment records, and any security deposit accounting.
  • Demand letters, court papers, and any prior stipulations or settlements.

When you request a consultation, identify whether you are the landlord, tenant, or guarantor and flag any upcoming court or notice deadline. Keep confidential documents for a later discussion after engagement is confirmed.

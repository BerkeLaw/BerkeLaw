Landlord–tenant disputes in Queens.

Landlord–tenant matters often move quickly and involve strict notice and pleading requirements. We represent parties in summary proceedings and related civil litigation to protect rights and resolve disputes efficiently.

Residential matters

Nonpayment and holdover proceedings, lease enforcement issues, and disputes over notices, rent demands, defenses, and stipulations.

Commercial matters

Lease defaults, enforcement, possession disputes, and litigation strategy focused on business realities and risk management.

Litigation and resolution

We assess the record, notices, and timeline, then pursue motion practice or negotiated resolutions when appropriate—always mindful of deadlines and procedural requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to appear in Housing Court?

Follow the instructions on the court notice or order. If you have a lawyer, ask whether you need to attend or whether your lawyer can appear for you. A tenant who misses a required appearance can face a default judgment.

What notices are required before filing?

New York law requires a written rent demand with at least 14 days’ notice before a nonpayment case can be filed. In a holdover case, the required notices depend on the reason for seeking possession, the lease, and applicable law. The timing and method of service matter.

What should I bring to a consultation?

Bring the lease and any renewals, notices and court papers, rent ledgers or receipts, and relevant photos or messages. A short timeline with service dates, court dates, and other deadlines helps put those records in context.

Can I negotiate a move-out date or payment plan?

Yes, if both sides agree. A written settlement, called a stipulation, can set a move-out date, a payment schedule, or both. The agreement should spell out the deadlines and what happens if either side misses one.

What if a prior stipulation was violated?

Check the signed stipulation for any required notice or time to fix the violation. Depending on its terms and the status of the case, either side may be able to ask the court for help, including through an order to show cause.

Do you handle both landlord and tenant matters?

Yes. The firm represents landlords and tenants in separate matters, subject to a conflict check and agreement on the terms of representation.

Deen v Holder (Sup Ct, Queens County 2021): $800,000 damages award, plus interest

Written and reviewed by Jason Berke, Esq., Law Offices of Jason Berke PC, Kew Gardens, Queens · Last reviewed November 7, 2026

In Mohamed Deen and Ashley Deen v. Hayden Holder, Index No. 701105/2020, Supreme Court, Queens County, Justice Timothy J. Dufficy conducted an inquest on October 21, 2021. After inquest, the court granted judgment in the amount of $700,000 for assault and emotional distress and $100,000 for loss of consortium. That is an $800,000 damages award before interest. The court directed that judgment be entered with interest from November 17, 2013. The entered judgment recites damages of $800,000.00, together with prejudgment statutory interest from November 17, 2013 in the amount of $578,761.64, and costs and disbursements of $1,143.60, for a total of $1,379,905.24. It states that the plaintiffs shall have execution thereof. The County Clerk entered the judgment on November 29, 2021, as NYSCEF Document 32. The certified inquest transcript lists Jason R. Berke, Esq., as counsel for the plaintiffs and records him examining both plaintiffs.

What was the proceeding about?

The captioned plaintiffs are Mohamed Deen and Ashley Deen. The defendant is Hayden Holder. The proceeding was an inquest after an order granting a default, which was marked as an exhibit. On the record, counsel described the claims as assault, battery, intentional infliction of emotional distress, and loss of consortium. Jason R. Berke examined both plaintiffs.

What did the court award?

Justice Dufficy stated: “After inquest, judgment is granted in the amount of $700,000 for the emotional distress and assault and in favor of the plaintiff, the loss of consortium for $100,000.” The court directed counsel to enter judgment with the clerk with the reporter’s minutes, and with interest from November 17, 2013. The court repeated that interest date and said, “That’s the order of the Court.” The $800,000 total is the sum of those two damages figures, before interest.

The entered judgment, dated November 29, 2021, recites that after the non-jury inquest the court rendered a verdict in favor of the plaintiffs in the sum of $800,000.00 in damages, together with prejudgment statutory interest from November 17, 2013 in the amount of $578,761.64, and costs and disbursements of $1,143.60, for a total of $1,379,905.24. It states that the plaintiffs shall have execution thereof. Queens County Supreme Court notified the parties that Document 32, the judgment, was entered on November 29, 2021, in MOHAMED DEEN et al v. HAYDEN HOLDER, Index No. 701105/2020, assigned to Justice Timothy J. Dufficy.

What did the decision not decide?

  • The award followed an inquest after an order granting a default. It is not a jury verdict after a fully contested trial on liability.
  • The $800,000 figure is the damages award before interest. The entered judgment separately states prejudgment statutory interest of $578,761.64 and a total of $1,379,905.24, including $1,143.60 in costs and disbursements.
  • This note does not restate the plaintiffs’ testimony about the underlying incident, and it does not add factual findings beyond the court’s award.
  • The certified transcript lists Jason R. Berke as counsel for the plaintiffs, appearing as Holihan & Associates, P.C. That is the appearance recorded at the inquest; it is not a listing for Law Offices of Jason Berke PC.
